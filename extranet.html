Extranet

An extranet is a controlled, private network that allows limited and secure access to an organization's intranet for authorized external parties such as suppliers, business partners, or clients — without exposing the full internal network.

What is an Extranet?

An extranet sits between the public Internet and a private intranet. It extends a carefully selected portion of an organization's internal network to trusted outside parties — without giving them full access to everything inside.

Access is tightly controlled through passwords, VPN connections, or security certificates, ensuring that only verified and authorized external users can enter the system.

Real World Examples of Extranets

  • A supplier logging into a retailer's stock ordering and inventory management system
  • A client accessing a law firm's case management or document sharing portal
  • A hospital securely sharing patient records and test results with a partner clinic or specialist
  • A university giving alumni limited access to certain library databases and academic resources
  • A construction company giving contractors access to project plans, schedules, and site documents

How an Extranet is Secured

Username and Password: Only users with valid credentials can log in to the extranet portal.

VPN (Virtual Private Network): Creates an encrypted tunnel between the external user and the organization's network, keeping data private during transmission.

Firewalls: Filter incoming and outgoing traffic to prevent unauthorized access while allowing legitimate users through.

SSL/TLS Encryption: Ensures that all data exchanged between the user and the extranet is encrypted and cannot be intercepted.

Comparing the Three Networks

Internet: Fully open to everyone worldwide with no access restrictions.

Intranet: Completely private and restricted to internal employees only.

Extranet: Selectively open — granting specific access to trusted external users using secure credentials and controlled permissions.
